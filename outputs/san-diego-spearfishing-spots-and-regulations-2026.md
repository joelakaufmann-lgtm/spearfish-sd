# San Diego Spearfishing: Best Areas, Seasons, Limits, and Location Advice

**Research snapshot:** August 5, 2026  
**Geographic scope:** San Diego County ocean waters, north to south  
**Audience:** Recreational freedivers and scuba divers taking finfish, with a short section on hand-taken lobster and invertebrates

> **Important:** This is a researched field guide, not a substitute for the regulations or real-time safety information. California can change groundfish, Pacific halibut, and other rules in-season. Marine protected area (MPA) boundaries are mostly unmarked offshore. Before every dive, check the [CDFW Ocean Sport Fishing Interactive Map](https://wildlife.ca.gov/OceanSportfishMap), the [current Southern Region regulations](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Fishing-Map/Southern), local access rules, the marine forecast, and [SD Beach Info](https://www.sdbeachinfo.com/).

## Quick answer

For most divers, the best San Diego **search areas** are:

1. **The open La Jolla corridor between Matlahuayl SMR and South La Jolla SMR** — the strongest all-around shore option for reef, kelp, and sand habitat, but the boundary work, shorebreak, crowds, and difficult exits make local knowledge important.
2. **Point Loma kelp beds outside Cabrillo SMR, by boat** — the best broad boat-based habitat, with excellent kelp and rocky structure; it is an advanced option because of current, traffic, entanglement, naval/security areas, and the no-take reserve at the tip.
3. **Open Oceanside and North County coast** — useful kelp, reef, and sand corridors outside the lagoons and Swami's SMCA; productive water is more spread out, and city-specific speargun rules must be checked.
4. **Leucadia/Encinitas north of Swami's northern boundary** — a good shore-search corridor with mixed reef, kelp, and sand; maintain a large buffer from the MPA and other water users.
5. **Del Mar, Torrey Pines, and Black's north of the Scripps boundary** — useful sand-edge and scattered-structure water, but access is strenuous and the Scripps MPA begins immediately to the south.
6. **Swami's SMCA itself** — a specialized pelagic/white-seabass option, **not** a general reef-fish spot. Only white seabass and specified pelagic finfish may be speared there.
7. **Coronado and Silver Strand oceanfront** — a lower-structure halibut-oriented option when water quality is fully open; avoid bay entrances, channels, and South Bay contamination advisories.

These are corridors, not secret reef coordinates. Conditions, safe access, exact MPA position, and the presence of other people matter more than the name of the beach.

## The four-gate rule

A fish is legal only if **all four gates** are open:

1. **The exact water is open to spearfishing.** A nearby legal beach does not make the offshore water legal.
2. **The species is legal by spear.** Protected species, zero-retention groundfish, and method restrictions still apply.
3. **The fish satisfies the current season, size, bag, and possession rules.** Use the live regulation page when it differs from a summary booklet.
4. **Local access and safety rules allow the dive.** City speargun ordinances, swimmer zones, harbor rules, park closures, surf, sewage closures, and security zones are separate legal and practical gates.

If any gate is uncertain, do not load the gun or take the animal.

## Detailed location guide, north to south

### 1. Oceanside open coast and offshore kelp

**Why it makes the list:** Oceanside has open coast without an ocean MPA and a mix of sand, artificial/hard structure, and kelp habitat. It is a useful search zone for divers who want more room than central San Diego often provides.

**Likely targets:** Kelp bass, barred sand bass, sheephead, California halibut, ocean whitefish, legal rockfish/greenling/cabezon, and—when present—white seabass, yellowtail, bonito, or barracuda.

**Best approach:**

- A properly operated boat or kayak can reach scattered kelp and hard-bottom structure without a long blind shore swim.
- For shore entry, use a guarded public beach corridor and ask the lifeguard where swimmers, surfers, and rip channels are concentrated.
- Search transitions rather than empty water: sand-to-reef edges, kelp edges, cuts, and bait-holding structure.

**Seasonal advice:** Late spring through fall usually offers the broadest mix of reef fish and pelagics. Winter can still be worthwhile during small-swell, clean-water windows, particularly for sand and reef species.

**Main hazards and limits:**

- Oceanside Harbor, its main channel, and its entrance are not shore-dive shortcuts; swimming there is prohibited and vessel traffic is intense.
- Groins, jetties, and surf zones create surge, hooks/line, and collision risk.
- Offshore visibility may differ sharply from the beach. Cancel if the exit, buddy, or float cannot be tracked reliably.

Useful local references: [Oceanside coastal management](https://www.ci.oceanside.ca.us/government/public-works/beaches-pier/coastal-management) and [Oceanside Lifeguards](https://fire.ci.oceanside.ca.us/department-overview/divisions/lifeguards).

### 2. Carlsbad and South Carlsbad open coast

**Why it makes the list:** The oceanfront is outside an ocean MPA; Batiquitos Lagoon's no-take protection is east of I-5, not a blanket closure of the Carlsbad shoreline. State Parks lists fishing and diving activities at [Carlsbad State Beach](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?lang=en&page_id=653) and [South Carlsbad State Beach](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?PAGE_ID=660).

**Likely targets:** California halibut over sand, kelp/barred sand bass around hard bottom, sheephead, whitefish, and seasonal pelagics where kelp or bait develops.

**Best approach:** Treat this as a broad search corridor rather than a guaranteed reef. Confirm visible kelp, bottom structure, access, and exit before committing to a long swim.

**Critical local-law caution:** Carlsbad Municipal Code §8.16.015 broadly prohibits discharging a projectile weapon on public property, including beaches, without permission. A State Parks page saying “fishing” or “skin diving” does not resolve speargun use. **Confirm the intended entry and speargun use with the responsible lifeguard/agency or Carlsbad authorities before a shore dive.** If confirmation is not clear, choose another location; do not improvise an exemption. See the [Carlsbad code](https://ecode360.com/44005232).

**Hazards:** Shorebreak, exposed stair access, shallow reef surge, longshore current, surfers, and limited exit options below bluffs.

### 3. Leucadia and Encinitas north of Swami's SMCA

**Why it makes the list:** Open water north of Swami's can provide reef, kelp, and sand habitat with practical North County shore access.

**Boundary rule:** Swami's SMCA reaches the shoreline at **33°02.900′ N**. The beach name is not the boundary. Check the pin for the entry, the entire planned hunt, and the return route in the [CDFW interactive map](https://wildlife.ca.gov/OceanSportfishMap), then keep a conservative buffer north of the line.

**Likely targets:** Kelp and sand bass, sheephead, California halibut, whitefish, legal groundfish, white seabass, and transient pelagics.

**Seasonal advice:** Spring and early summer are commonly watched for white seabass; summer and fall can add yellowtail, bonito, and barracuda. Reef and sand species remain potential targets year-round when the water is safe and visible.

**Local rule:** Encinitas prohibits a cocked/armed speargun or unsheathed spear within **100 feet of another person on public property**. Keep the gun unloaded and the spear covered during transport, use an uncrowded entry, and do not load near surfers or swimmers. See [Encinitas Municipal Code §8.04.020](https://ecode360.com/44477417).

**Hazards:** Crowded surf breaks, shallow reef, poor visibility after swell, and boundary drift from current. A float does not justify hunting through a surf lineup.

### 4. Swami's SMCA and Cardiff reefs — restricted specialist option

**Why it makes the list:** Swami's is productive-looking reef and kelp habitat, but it is not an ordinary reef-fishing zone. It is the **only San Diego County MPA that permits any recreational spearfishing**.

**What may be speared in the MPA:** White seabass and specified pelagic finfish, including practical San Diego targets such as yellowtail, Pacific bonito, barracuda, dorado, mackerels, and tunas. The area rule also names some species that statewide law does not allow divers to spear; the statewide method restriction still controls.

**What may not be taken by spear there:** Kelp bass, barred/spotted sand bass, sheephead, California halibut, rockfish, cabezon, greenling, scorpionfish, lobster, scallops, octopus, and other reef fish or invertebrates.

**Boundaries:** The shoreline section runs from **33°02.900′ N** south to **33°00.000′ N**, with San Elijo Lagoon separately protected near its mouth. The SMCA extends well offshore, so swimming farther out does not escape it.

**Advice:**

- Enter only with strong fish identification and a deliberate pelagic-only plan.
- Do not shoot a fish simply because it is swimming in midwater; legal status depends on species, not behavior.
- Stay clear of surfers and swimmers. The practical safe-to-hunt area may be much smaller than the legal area.
- If the intended target is bass, sheephead, halibut, lobster, or a groundfish, dive elsewhere.

Official source: [Swami's SMCA and San Elijo Lagoon SMCA](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Swamis-San-Elijo-Lagoon).

### 5. Del Mar, Torrey Pines, and Black's north of Scripps SMCA

**Why it makes the list:** This stretch offers sand channels, scattered reef, and kelp-edge opportunities, with less concentrated reef than La Jolla proper. It is especially relevant to divers looking for legal-sized halibut rather than only dense kelp structure.

**Boundary traps:**

- San Dieguito Lagoon SMCA is not a dive entry; swimming, wading, boating, and diving are prohibited there, and the lagoon is closed 8 p.m.–5 a.m.
- San Diego-Scripps Coastal SMCA begins at the shoreline at **32°53.000′ N** and allows only limited hook-and-line take—not spearfishing.
- Black's Beach is immediately north of the Scripps boundary. A small navigation error can put the diver inside the SMCA.

**Likely targets:** California halibut on sand and sand/reef transitions; kelp or sand bass, sheephead, whitefish, white seabass, and yellowtail where suitable structure and bait occur.

**Best approach:** Use this corridor only after plotting the southern limit and the farthest intended swim. For halibut, make slow parallel passes over sand channels and edges; identify both eyes, mouth orientation, and tail before shooting a flatfish.

**Access and hazard advice:** [Black's Beach](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/beaches/blacks) has an unstable roughly 300-foot bluff, unimproved trails, tide-sensitive shore approaches, and remote exits. This is an advanced shore-access choice. Do not plan a dive whose only exit becomes unusable with rising tide or swell.

### 6. The open La Jolla corridor — best all-around shore area

**Where “open La Jolla” actually is:** The generally open corridor lies between:

- the southern shoreline boundary of **Matlahuayl SMR at about 32°51.067′ N**, and
- the northern shoreline boundary of **South La Jolla SMR at 32°49.573′ N**.

That narrow gap includes portions of the coast around Children's Pool, Marine Street, and Windansea, but the **exact pin and planned offshore swim must be checked**. Do not treat “La Jolla” as a single legal zone.

**Why it ranks first:** Within a relatively compact corridor, divers can find kelp, rocky reef, ledges, sand channels, and open-water edges. This creates opportunities for reef, sand, and pelagic species without requiring a boat.

**Likely targets:** Kelp bass, sheephead, California halibut, ocean whitefish, legal groundfish, white seabass, yellowtail, bonito, and barracuda.

**Where to look:**

- Kelp bass and sheephead: broken reef, kelp stalks, ledges, and reef/sand interfaces.
- Halibut: clean sand, especially channels and edges near structure—not blindly inside eelgrass or dense reef.
- White seabass: quiet kelp lanes and edges where bait is present; minimize surface splashing and unnecessary drops.
- Yellowtail/bonito: outer kelp, bait schools, current lines, and blue-water edges, only within the diver's depth and recovery capacity.

**Seasonal advice:** Spring into early summer is the classic white-seabass watch period, but March 15–June 15 also carries the reduced one-fish white-seabass limit. Summer and fall more often add warm-water pelagics. Reef fish and halibut are legal year-round, subject to size/bag rules and conditions.

**Access cautions:**

- [Windansea](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/beaches/windan) can have moderate-to-severe shorebreak over reef.
- [Children's Pool](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/beaches/pool) beach is closed **December 15–May 15** and is known for strong current and high-surf hazards. A nearby sidewalk does not create legal beach access during the closure.
- [Point La Jolla and Boomer Beach](https://www.sandiego.gov/park-and-recreation/point-lajolla) are under a year-round public closure; the designated ocean-access corridor is for swimming, not spearfishing. They are also associated with the no-take Matlahuayl reserve.
- City law restricts armed spearguns near swimmers. In practice, a busy day can make a nominally open spot unusable.

**Skill level:** An experienced local buddy is strongly recommended for the first dives. The challenge is not merely depth; it is combining boundary navigation, shorebreak timing, kelp, crowds, seals/sea lions, and a reliable exit.

Official MPA sources: [San Diego-Scripps Coastal SMCA and Matlahuayl SMR](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/San-Diego-Scripps-Coastal-Matlahuayl) and [South La Jolla SMR/SMCA](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/South-La-Jolla).

### 7. Pacific Beach, Mission Beach, Ocean Beach, and Sunset Cliffs

**MPA boundary:** South La Jolla's shoreward reserve extends to **32°47.945′ N**. Bird Rock, False Point, Tourmaline, and north Pacific Beach waters are inside or close enough to the reserve that a live map check is mandatory. The offshore companion SMCA also prohibits spearfishing.

**Central Pacific Beach/Mission Beach:** Mostly sand with isolated hard bottom. This is more logically a halibut or opportunistic search area than a premier reef destination. Swimmer, surfer, and board traffic often eliminates a safe hunting lane.

**Ocean Beach/open water:** Mixed sand and rock can hold bass, sheephead, halibut, and seasonal pelagics, but avoid the pier, jetties, navigation channels, swimmers, and surf zones. Confirm local closure and construction conditions before entry.

**Sunset Cliffs north of Cabrillo SMR:** Rocky, kelp-influenced water can be attractive, but the coast has steep cliffs, caves, surge, and poor exits. [Sunset Cliffs Natural Park](https://www.sandiego.gov/park-and-recreation/parks/regional/shoreline/sunset) is principally a bluff/viewpoint park, not a promise of safe dive access. Use only a documented, currently open entry and exit that works at the expected tide and swell. This is an advanced choice; boat access is often more defensible.

**City rule:** Throughout City of San Diego public swimming areas, do not carry a cocked/armed speargun where swimmers are present or within 50 feet of a swimmer. Spears/barbs must be covered, sheathed, or removed during transport. See the local-law section below.

### 8. Point Loma kelp beds outside Cabrillo SMR — best boat option

**Why it ranks highly:** Point Loma's outer kelp and rocky habitat can combine reef fish, white seabass, and passing pelagics. Boat access lets a team choose a legal, uncrowded drop and preserve a clean exit.

**Legal boundary:** The kelp and rocky habitat at the Point Loma tip inside **Cabrillo SMR is no-take**. The reserve's shoreline spans approximately **32°40.600′ N to 32°39.700′ N** and projects offshore. Plot the full polygon, not only the park boundary. [Cabrillo National Monument](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/management/compendium.htm) also prohibits swimming, wading, snorkeling, diving, and boating from park property.

**Likely targets outside the reserve:** Kelp bass, sheephead, whitefish, legal groundfish, white seabass, yellowtail, bonito, and barracuda.

**Boat plan:**

- The operator remains responsible for vessel control; do not abandon a drifting boat without a competent plan.
- Mark the diver group with a highly visible float/flag and choose a drop outside shipping, Navy, harbor, and security areas.
- Account for current before entry. Plan pickup down-current without forcing exhausted divers to swim to the vessel.
- Keep lines, shooting line, kelp, and stringers away from the body. Carry two accessible cutting options when practical.
- If groundfish will be possessed aboard, have the required descending device available even though a spear diver normally avoids barotrauma releases.

**Skill level:** Advanced. Current, boat traffic, kelp entanglement, deeper structure, and long surface swims compound quickly.

Official source: [Cabrillo SMR](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Cabrillo).

### 9. Coronado and Silver Strand oceanfront

**Why it is included:** The oceanfront is outside a CDFW MPA and offers long sandy-bottom corridors. [Coronado Beach](https://www.coronado.ca.us/244/Coronado-Beach) and [Silver Strand State Beach](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=654) recognize fishing/diving activities subject to other law.

**Best use:** California halibut search over sand and near subtle depressions, sand dollars, bait, or isolated structure. It is not the county's premier kelp or reef-fish zone.

**Critical water-quality gate:** Check [SD Beach Info](https://www.sdbeachinfo.com/) the same day. South Bay warnings and closures may result from sewage, elevated bacteria, Tijuana River flow, or transboundary contamination that is not visible or detectable by smell. A legal fishery is not a safe dive when the water is under warning or closure.

**Hazards:** Rip currents, shorebreak, longshore current, limited underwater landmarks, low visibility, and traffic near bay/navigation entrances. Stay well away from shipping and harbor approaches.

### 10. Imperial Beach and Tijuana River mouth — exclude from the shortlist

Imperial Beach is not a practical shore-spearfishing recommendation:

- Imperial Beach Municipal Code Chapter 12.60 prohibits using a fishing spear or speargun within **1,000 feet of the shoreline**. See the [city code](https://ecode360.com/42864480).
- Tijuana River Mouth SMCA extends from **32°34.000′ N** south to the international border and prohibits spearfishing.
- Water-quality closures and warnings are recurrent and can involve contamination that is invisible and odorless.

Do not confuse Coronado or Silver Strand with **Los Coronados Islands**, which are in Mexico and require compliance with Mexican entry, boating, and fishing law.

### Areas that should not be treated as spearfishing spots

- **La Jolla Cove, Point La Jolla, Boomers, and Matlahuayl SMR:** no take.
- **Scripps and much of La Jolla Shores:** San Diego-Scripps Coastal SMCA; no spearfishing.
- **Bird Rock, False Point, Tourmaline/north PB, and South La Jolla waters:** no-take reserve or no-spearfishing offshore SMCA; verify any claimed open edge precisely.
- **Point Loma tip/Cabrillo:** no-take reserve and separate National Park access rules.
- **Batiquitos, San Elijo, and San Dieguito lagoons:** protected; diving or water entry is prohibited or severely restricted.
- **Famosa Slough:** no take.
- **Mission Bay and San Diego Bay:** do not assume open-water fishing rules authorize a spear dive. Swimmer zones, channels, harbor rules, military/commercial traffic, and permit requirements control. The [Port of San Diego Harbor Police](https://www.portofsandiego.org/public-safety/harbor-police-faq) requires permits for most underwater diving in San Diego Bay.
- **Oceanside Harbor and entrance:** no swimming; high vessel risk.
- **Tijuana River Mouth and Imperial Beach nearshore:** no spearfishing and major water-quality concerns.

## Seasonal field calendar

Legal seasons and fish opportunity are different. The table below describes **typical planning patterns**, not guarantees.

| Period | Common opportunity | Conditions and strategy | Legal reminders |
|---|---|---|---|
| January–February | Reef fish, sheephead, halibut, legal groundfish; occasional white-seabass opportunity | Cold water, northwest swells, storms, runoff, and short visibility windows. Favor small-swell mornings and protected, known exits. | Spear divers may take sheephead/groundfish year-round under diver exceptions; if using a boat during a boat-based closure, only spearfishing gear may be aboard. Lobster is normally in season through mid-March, but is hand-take only. |
| March–April | White seabass interest rises; halibut move into shallower coastal habitat; reef fish remain available | Upwelling can make water cold and green; visibility can change by the hour. Listen for croaking but do not chase depth or breath-hold limits. | White seabass drops to **1 fish March 15–June 15** south of Point Conception. Pacific halibut opens April 1 unless changed/closed. Lobster closes in mid-March. |
| May–mid-June | Often the most watched white-seabass window; halibut, bass, sheephead, and whitefish | Kelp and bait conditions matter more than the date. Enter quietly, reduce surface disturbance, and work within a buddy-observed depth. | White seabass remains **1** through June 15. Children's Pool beach remains closed through May 15. |
| Mid-June–August | Yellowtail, bonito, barracuda, and other pelagics become more likely; reef fish and halibut continue | Warmer water, south swells, red tide, dense beach use, and boat traffic. Early uncrowded windows are preferable. Offshore tuna/dorado require a capable vessel and blue-water safety plan. | White seabass returns to **3** after June 15. Check current Pacific-halibut and groundfish notices. |
| September–November | Often the best all-around blend of warmer water, settled mornings, pelagics, and reef opportunity | Pelagics may linger; visibility can be excellent but is not guaranteed. Santa Ana/offshore-wind events and sudden swell changes require forecast review. | 2026–27 lobster season opens **Friday, October 2 at 6:00 p.m.** Lobster is hand-only for divers, with gauge and report card. |
| December | Reef fish, halibut, legal groundfish, and lobster during calm windows | Storm swell, runoff, cooling water, and limited daylight narrow the safe days. Build in a conservative turnaround time. | Children's Pool beach closes December 15. Pacific halibut's live 2026 season runs through December 31 south of Point Arena unless quota action closes it earlier. |

California halibut adults generally move into shallower coastal water to spawn from roughly February through September, which supports spring-through-fall sand searches; see CDFW's [California halibut species information](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Nearshore/1000). Local sport-fishing patterns also place much of San Diego's pelagic opportunity in spring through fall, but boat catch reports are context—not proof that a shore location will hold fish.

### What matters more than the month

1. **Swell height, direction, period, and trend.** A building long-period swell can make a benign entry dangerous before a forecast headline looks dramatic.
2. **Wind and current.** Morning calm can disappear; plan the return before entering.
3. **Water quality.** Rain, sewage releases, and Tijuana River flow can close or contaminate water regardless of visibility.
4. **Visibility at hunting depth.** Surface clarity is not enough. If buddies cannot maintain direct supervision, abort.
5. **Crowding.** A legal reef inside a surf lineup or swimmer zone is not a usable spearfishing location.
6. **Bait and habitat.** Fish respond to food, water mass, kelp condition, and current—not a calendar promise.

## 2026 take limits for common San Diego targets

### How to read this table

- These are the rules verified for San Diego on **August 5, 2026**.
- The default possession limit is generally one daily bag unless a specific rule allows otherwise. Frozen or preserved fish still count toward possession.
- “Total length” is measured from the tip of the closed mouth to the longest tail lobe. “Fork length” ends at the center of the tail fork. “Alternate length” uses the base of the foremost first-dorsal spine to the longest tail lobe.
- Unless a specific exception applies, the fallback is **20 finfish total, with no more than 10 of one species**.
- This table covers the realistic and legally significant San Diego spear targets; for any unlisted species, check the regulation before taking it.

| Species/group | Spear-diver season in San Diego | Daily bag / general possession | Minimum size | Key note |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Kelp bass, barred sand bass, spotted sand bass | Year-round | **5 combined**; no more than **4 barred sand bass** | 14 in total or 10 in alternate length | The barred-bass sublimit is scheduled to sunset June 1, 2028 unless changed. |
| California sheephead | Year-round for shore/spear divers | **2** | 12 in total | Boat anglers close Jan–Feb; a diver using a boat then may have only spearfishing gear aboard. |
| White seabass | Year-round | **1** south of Point Conception March 15–June 15; otherwise **3** | 28 in total or **20.5 in alternate length** | Use the 28-in whole-fish standard; a live summary has inconsistent alternate-length text. |
| Yellowtail | Year-round | **10** | 24 in fork, but up to 5 under 24 in may be taken/possessed | Count the undersize allowance carefully; it is not 10 small fish. |
| California halibut | Year-round | **5** south of Point Sur | 22 in total | Learn halibut vs. other flatfish before shooting. |
| Pacific halibut | **April 1–December 31, 2026** south of Point Arena, or earlier quota closure | **1** | None | The live CDFW page controls; the July booklet retained an older November 15 date. |
| Lingcod | Year-round for spear divers under the groundfish exception | **2** | 22 in total | MPA, zero-retention, and GEA rules still apply. |
| Rockfish + cabezon + kelp/rock greenling (RCG complex) | Year-round for spear divers under the groundfish exception | **10 combined** | None currently | Within 10: copper 1; canary 2; vermilion/sunset 2. Bronzespotted, cowcod, quillback, and yelloweye are zero retention. |
| California scorpionfish (“sculpin”) | Year-round/all depths outside GEAs | **5** | None currently | Venomous spines remain dangerous after death; handle deliberately. |
| Ocean whitefish | Year-round/all depths | **10**, within general 20 | None | Do not confuse with protected or zero-retention species. |
| California barracuda | Year-round | **10**, within general 20 | 28 in total or 17 in alternate length | Long, slender fish are easy to underestimate underwater. |
| Pacific bonito | Year-round | **10** | 24 in fork or 5 lb; up to 5 may be under both thresholds | Bleed/chill promptly for food quality where lawful and safe. |
| Dorado/dolphinfish | Year-round | **10**, within general 20 | None | Primarily a vessel/blue-water target; use a dedicated offshore safety plan. |
| Albacore | Year-round | **10** south of Point Conception, in addition to general 20 | None | Tuna filleting/labeling rules apply aboard south of Point Conception. |
| Bluefin tuna | Year-round | **2**, in addition to general 20 | None | Check federal/current notices before an offshore trip. |
| Skipjack tuna | Year-round | No numerical limit | None | Do not confuse it with other tuna subject to a 10-fish or 2-fish limit. |
| Yellowfin, bigeye, and other tuna | Year-round | **10**, within general 20 | None | Confirm species before counting it under the correct tuna rule. |

|---|---:|---:|---|
| Leopard shark | 3 | 36 in total | Low reproductive output and identification issues argue for restraint even where lawful. |
| Shortfin mako, thresher, blue shark | 2 of each species | None listed | High-risk blue-water target; do not attempt without specialist training, gear, boat support, and positive identification. |
| Sixgill and sevengill shark | 1 of each species | None listed | Rare/slow-growing; positive identification is essential. |
| Soupfin shark | 1 | None listed | Counts under the other-groundfish framework; GEAs apply. |
| White shark | **0—no take** | Not applicable | Release immediately without removing from water if incidentally taken; do not attract or target. |

The legal maximum is not a harvest recommendation. Large breeding fish and slow-growing species can matter disproportionately. Many experienced divers voluntarily take less than the limit.

### Protected and no-spear species

Do not spear or possess:

- Giant (black) sea bass
- Garibaldi
- Gulf grouper or broomtail grouper
- White shark
- Bronzespotted, cowcod, quillback, or yelloweye rockfish
- Green sturgeon
- Any other species carrying a current zero-retention rule

California's spearfishing method rule also bars taking **trout, salmon, and broadbill swordfish by spear**, even if an MPA's allowed-species list uses a broader pelagic definition.

When uncertain between a legal and protected species, do not shoot. Identification after the shaft lands is too late.

### The groundfish diver exception—what it does and does not do

Under 14 CCR §27.20(b)(1)(D), spear divers may generally take lawful RCG species, lingcod, and covered federal groundfish **year-round and without the ordinary boat/angling depth schedule**.

It does **not** override:

- MPA rules;
- species size or bag limits;
- zero-retention species;
- Groundfish Exclusion Areas (GEAs);
- local access/speargun law; or
- a boat-gear restriction during an otherwise closed boat season.

If a boat is used during a boat-based groundfish closure, only spearfishing gear may be aboard. The offshore **43-Fathom Spot GEA** is relevant to San Diego; all groundfish take/possession is closed inside it. Use the official [groundfish summary/map resources](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Groundfish-Summary) and do not rely on a casual chart label.

Any vessel taking or possessing groundfish must have a descending device available for immediate use. This applies operationally even if the spear diver expects no hook-and-line releases.

### Measurement, catch ownership, and handling

- Measure before retaining. Carry a rigid gauge/tape that starts at zero; do not use estimated shaft bands or fin markings.
- Keep size-limited fish whole until ashore whenever practical. Vessel fillet rules are species-specific and preserve identifying skin; an incorrect fillet can make a legal fish unlawful.
- Do not assume a boat may pool spear-diver limits. Segregate and identify each diver's catch and keep each diver within individual limits.
- Catch, licenses, report cards, and take gear must be shown to a wildlife officer on demand.
- Do not transport live finfish away from the water where taken.
- Bleed and ice retained fish promptly when safe. A float-mounted catch bag or stringer is preferable to attaching struggling fish directly to the diver.

### Lobster operational checklist

1. Obtain the physical lobster report card even if under 16 or otherwise license-exempt.
2. Before entering the water, record the date, location code, and gear in indelible ink.
3. Carry a compliant gauge; measure each lobster immediately **in the water** from the rear edge of the eye socket to the rear edge of the body shell.
4. Never place an undersize lobster in a bag “to measure later.”
5. Take crustaceans by hand only while diving; no hooked device may aid the take.
6. Record retained lobster as required when moving locations or ending the session.
7. Report/return the card by April 30 under the current instructions, even if no lobster was retained.
8. Recheck the [CDFW lobster page](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Invertebrates/Lobster) before opening night.

### How to verify a boundary in the field

1. Open the [CDFW Ocean Sport Fishing Interactive Map](https://wildlife.ca.gov/OceanSportfishMap) and enable device location.
2. Tap the **water**, not the parking lot, and read the MPA popup.
3. Check the entry, the farthest intended hunting point, and the return route.
4. Compare the map with the written coordinates in 14 CCR §632 or the linked official MPA page.
5. Maintain a conservative buffer; GPS accuracy, current, and the diver's position can all vary.
6. Save an offline screenshot/map before entering, but recheck the live rule before the trip.

The [CDFW MPA FAQ](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/FAQs) explains that most boundaries are not marked with buoys and compliance remains the user's responsibility. If transiting a no-spearfishing MPA is unavoidable, follow the exact transit rule: remain at the surface, keep gear unloaded and not carried in hand, and do not hunt or deploy gear. A boat may transit with lawfully taken catch only while fishing gear is not deployed.

## Licenses, validations, and local law

### State documents

- Anyone **16 or older** attempting to take fish or invertebrates needs a California sport-fishing license.
- San Diego ocean fishing generally requires an **Ocean Enhancement Validation** on a 365-day or 10-day license; one- and two-day licenses are exempt from that validation.
- The CDFW License App may display a license and validation, but physical report cards remain required.
- A shore diver may generally leave the license within 500 yards of shore; a boat diver may leave it aboard. Carrying it securely is simpler.
- A lobster report card is required for every participant, including people under 16 or otherwise license-exempt.
- Night ocean diving is generally possible, but [Fish and Game Code §2005](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=2005.&lawCode=FGC) restricts artificial light used on or as part of fishing tackle and prohibits night-vision equipment. Keep the dive light independent of the speargun/tackle and check the current text before a night hunt.

Official sources: [CDFW sport-fishing licenses and report cards](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Fishing/Free-Licenses), [CDFW License App](https://wildlife.ca.gov/licenseapp), and [California Fish and Game Code license provisions](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=3.&chapter=1.&division=6.&lawCode=FGC&part=2.&title=).

## Safety system for San Diego diving

### Non-negotiables

1. **Never freedive alone.** Use one-up/one-down diving with a trained buddy directly supervising the entire ascent and at least the first 30 seconds after surfacing.
2. **Never hyperventilate.** It suppresses the urge to breathe without safely adding meaningful oxygen and can cause blackout without warning.
3. **Match the dive to the weaker team member.** Predetermine maximum depth, separation, turnaround time, lost-buddy procedure, and exit.
4. **Keep the airway observable.** A buddy on the other side of opaque kelp, glare, or low visibility is not providing rescue-ready supervision.
5. **Weight for surface safety.** Use a quick-release belt and remain positively buoyant at an appropriate shallow depth for the exposure suit and plan.
6. **Carry cutting tools.** Kelp, shooting line, discarded monofilament, lobster gear, and float line can entangle. At least one tool must be reachable with either hand; two are preferable in dense kelp.
7. **Do not attach struggling catch to the body.** Use a float or board positioned so it does not tangle the diver.
8. **Unload before shore, rocks, boats, swimmer areas, and boundary transit.** Treat every loaded speargun as a firearm-equivalent directional hazard.
9. **Abort early.** A marginal entry becomes a worse exit when tired, cold, carrying fish, or surprised by a rising swell.

Divers Alert Network provides a useful overview of [freediving safety awareness](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/freediving-safety-awareness/). The [San Diego Lifeguards scuba safety page](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/safety/scuba) also emphasizes buddy diving, kelp-entanglement planning, a knife, and canceling in hazardous conditions.

### Surf, rip, and exit advice

- Watch at least several complete wave sets before entering. Identify where whitewater, reef boil, and longshore current send a disabled diver.
- Never assume the entry remains the best exit. Name a primary and backup exit before the first descent.
- In a rip current, do not exhaust yourself fighting directly toward shore; signal, float, and move parallel to escape the narrow current when able. San Diego Lifeguards note that rip currents are involved in a large majority of rescues. See [rip-current safety](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/safety/ripcurrent).
- Bluff entries such as Black's and Sunset Cliffs can be physically impossible or closed at the return tide. Treat cliff fall, rockfall, and route loss as separate hazards from the water.
- Call the San Diego Lifeguards recorded beach/surf and dive-conditions line, **619-221-8824**, and consult the [lifeguard page](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/), but make an independent site assessment.

### Boat and visibility advice

- Use a conspicuous float and diver-down flag even though California does not make the flag a universal statutory requirement. The symbol is recognized and its use is strongly recommended; it does not create an exclusive safety zone or guarantee a boat sees the diver.
- Never depend on hearing an approaching boat underwater.
- Keep the group compact enough for the operator to count and recover every diver.
- For blue-water hunting, use a purpose-built float/line system, competent skipper, positive diver accounting, and a plan for sharks, current, entanglement, and a disabled boat.
- Do not spear when visibility prevents positive species identification and a safe background. A shaft passing through or missing a fish can travel well beyond the target.

California's official boating-course material discusses the diver-down flag and recommended vessel clearance: [DBW California Boating Course PDF](https://dbw.parks.ca.gov/pages/28702/files/dbwboatcourse.pdf).

### Wildlife interactions

- Do not feed, corner, touch, or pursue seals or sea lions. Leave an area if an animal persistently challenges for catch.
- Keep fish off the body and under control. If a large shark shows focused/aggressive behavior, face it, stay with the buddy, avoid frantic splashing, and make a controlled exit.
- Garibaldi and giant sea bass are protected even when abundant and approachable.
- Do not reach blindly into holes. Moray eels, lobster gear, hooks, and venomous scorpionfish create hand injuries.

- **Local beach conditions:** [San Diego Lifeguards](https://www.sandiego.gov/lifeguards/) or the relevant North County agency

Never enter water under a sewage closure. A warning/advisory deserves the same conservative treatment for an immersion activity involving eyes, ears, mouth, cuts, and retained seafood. Contamination may not be visible or smell unusual.

### Consumption advisories

Legal bag limits are not dietary safety limits. Species, size, age, and location affect mercury and contaminant exposure. Consult:

- [OEHHA statewide advisory for California coastal locations without site-specific advice](https://oehha.ca.gov/fish/advisories/statewide-advisory-eating-fish-california-coastal-locations-without-site-specific-advice)

Children and people who are pregnant or may become pregnant should follow the advisory's more protective meal guidance. Do not use visual appearance or “freshness” to infer contaminant safety.

## Practical trip templates

### First San Diego shore dive

- Join an experienced local buddy rather than selecting a difficult entry from a map.
- Choose an open corridor with a guarded, uncrowded entry and modest surf—not Black's, Sunset Cliffs, or the edge of an MPA.
- Leave the gun unloaded until well clear of people; make the first session a navigation/safety dive if needed.
- Set a shallow depth cap and a short turnaround. Retaining no fish is a successful first reconnaissance.

Local groups with published safety/ethics materials include [San Diego Freedivers](https://www.sandiegofreedivers.com/pages/code-of-ethics) and [North County Depth Finders](https://www.northcountydepthfinders.com/). Club participation is not a substitute for formal freedive/rescue training.

### Halibut-focused shore dive

- Favor legal sand corridors near—not inside—reef/kelp edges.
- Use slow, parallel search passes with a buddy visible above; avoid stirring the bottom.
- Confirm species and 22-inch total length before retaining California halibut.
- Coronado/Silver Strand requires a same-day water-quality check; Del Mar/Black's requires exact boundary and exit planning.

### White-seabass kelp dive

- Recheck whether the date falls within the March 15–June 15 one-fish period.
- Work quiet kelp lanes/edges with bait rather than repeatedly extending depth/time.
- Use a floatline or breakaway setup that can be managed without tying the diver into kelp.
- Take only a high-confidence broadside shot within range; a lost fish is both an ethical failure and an entanglement hazard.

### Point Loma boat dive

- Plot Cabrillo SMR and security/navigation exclusions before departure.
- Assign a competent boat operator and maintain a written diver count.
- Confirm current direction, pickup plan, VHF/communications, first-aid/oxygen capability, and lost-diver procedure.
- Keep the required descending device aboard if any groundfish may be possessed.
- A mixed rod-and-spear trip can defeat the diver groundfish exception during a boat-based closure; check the gear rule before loading the vessel.

### Opening-night lobster dive

- Avoid the most crowded entries and never combine a loaded speargun with close-quarters lobster traffic.
- Complete the report card before water entry and carry a gauge.
- Use one-up/one-down supervision even on shallow night dives.
- Mark the exit with a legal, non-confusing shore light and carry backups without using night-vision equipment.
- Take lobster only by hand, measure underwater, and leave undersize animals where found.

- [ ] Everyone is willing to abort without debate

## Known 2026 source discrepancies and update risk

1. **White seabass alternate length:** The controlling regulation text is **20.5 inches alternate length**, while one live Southern summary displays a conflicting number. Use the unambiguous **28-inch total length** standard on a whole fish.
2. **Pacific halibut closing date:** The July booklet retained **November 15**, but the later live CDFW page and 2026 federal conforming measure set **December 31 south of Point Arena**, including San Diego, unless the quota closes earlier.
3. **Pending rulemaking:** Amendments affecting several finfish sections were proposed for action after this report's August 5 snapshot. A proposed rule is not yet an effective rule, but it is a reason to recheck before every trip.
4. **Local conditions and access:** Beach closures, construction, lifeguard directions, sewage events, and harbor/security restrictions can change without a change to the statewide fishing booklet.

**Bottom line:** Use this report to choose a promising legal corridor and prepare the trip. Use the live official sources to decide whether to enter and whether to take a particular fish that day.
